Fantasy Premier League points projections — public scorecard

Every projection below was written to disk before its gameweek deadline, alongside the SHA-256 of the exact API snapshot it was built from. This page shows how those projections actually performed — including the weeks they were beaten by a three-line baseline.

What we are committing to, in advance

We publish a projection for every player before every deadline, and we score it against the same two baselines every week: a player’s mean score over his last six gameweeks, and his points-per-game to date. The headline comparison is against the last-six baseline, because it is the harder one and beating only the easier one would not be a real claim. Weeks we lose are published in this table in the same style as weeks we win.

ρ is Spearman rank correlation between projected and actual points across the eligible player pool. A gameweek stays pending until its results are final. Made is how long before the deadline the scored projection was written; any earlier horizons also on record are listed after it. We publish from three days out and re-project as team news lands — the scored entry is always the last one before the deadline, and the earlier ones stay on the record so you can see how much the projection improves as news arrives.

Check this yourself. Each row’s snapshot column is the SHA-256 of the API response the projection was built from, recorded before the deadline. Running verify.py re-derives every published projection from its own snapshot and confirms the output is identical; it reports fail if an input was altered, and drift if the model has been refitted since. We do not ask to be taken on trust.

What we knew before launch

Backtested walk-forward across past seasons under a strict as-of cutoff: for each gameweek the model sees only earlier gameweeks plus the prior season. A truncation test — delete every later row, re-run, require byte-identical output — passes on 38/38 gameweeks across two seasons.

Known limitations

  • Opening gameweeks are the weakest. Before the season has data, projections lean on last season and on price. Expect the largest errors in August.
  • The bonus-point term is stale. FPL re-specified BPS for 2026/27; our term was fitted on the previous formula, so defender bonus is likely over-projected until it is refit on this season’s data.
  • No news ingestion yet. Injury and rotation news reaches the model only through FPL’s own availability flags.
  • Single-gameweek football is mostly noise. Even a perfect model would correlate only modestly with one week’s actual points. Judge any projection service over a season, not over a gameweek — including this one.
